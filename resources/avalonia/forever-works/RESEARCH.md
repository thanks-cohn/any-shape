# Research: preventing cross-target failure in a coordinated run

Distinguish **negative transfer** (inappropriate reuse causing worse results), **cross-target assumption leakage** (project operational term), **specification drift**, **architecture erosion**, **task/context interference**, **software product-line variability**, and **evidence laundering** (promoting untested claims). Long-context position effects are another reason to keep target constraints indexed and retrievable. These are hypotheses about agent workflows, not diagnoses of hidden cognition.

Investigate:
- H1: frozen neutral contracts plus strict host boundaries reduce wrong-platform assumptions versus prompt-only one-run generation.
- H2: selective per-platform retrieval reduces omitted obligations versus an undifferentiated context dump.
- H3: one boundary-enforced coordinated run can lower total turns/time relative to three independent runs without lowering independent target acceptance.

Compare unstructured single run, structure-enforced single run, and staged separate runs over identical synthetic extensions. Record per-target runtime passes/fails/unknowns, leaked imports, false transfer decisions, time/compute, human interventions, UI usability and effect fidelity. Keep tests independent of the agent-generated result. Do not declare success from source compilation alone.

Research context: Liu et al., *Lost in the Middle* (TACL 2024), https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/; Carnegie Mellon SEI software-product-line variability research; negative transfer learning survey literature. Further definitions and evidence standards: ../../../forever-works/RESEARCH_DRIFT_TRANSFER_AND_TARGET_ISOLATION.md.
